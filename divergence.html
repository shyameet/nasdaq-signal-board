How it works

What this page shows

The Signals page runs the same four rules on two series: the 10 biggest Nasdaq-100 stocks combined (NDX10) and the Nasdaq-100 itself (XYZ100). They agree about three quarters of the time. This page marks only the moments they disagree; when they agree, the chart stays clean.

The two patterns

Index alone: the index turned bullish while the 10 stocks didn't. In a 3-day study, XYZ100 was lower 15 minutes later about 7 times out of 10, on every day. 10 stocks first: the 10 stocks turned bullish while the index hadn't yet. XYZ100 was higher 30 minutes later about 7 times out of 10, but less consistently. Other differences showed no clear pattern.

The scoreboard

It replays the history loaded on this page (about two days online, three and a half on the local version) and counts how often each mark was followed by the expected move. The gray line on each bar is how often XYZ100 moved that way anyway, at any minute: a pattern is only useful if it beats that line. New marks are added as they happen. A few days is not much data, so treat the patterns as hints.
